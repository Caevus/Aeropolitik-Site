What Is Aeropolitik?

By Henry Reynard

Aeropolitik follows Edwin Leyendecker, a veteran turned police officer of the floating city of Helena. After being selected for the illustrious flying Sentinel Corps, and with freshly minted uniform and rocket pack equipped, Edwin feels on top of the world. His romantic dream comes crashing down, however, as his position on high allows him to see behind the curtain at the corruption, incompetence, and bad actors which run the city. On the horizon, a thunderstorm brews as the election of the century threatens to tumult the city's very core and send it spiraling into chaos and autocracy.

Under the surface, it's a story about institutional corruption, bureaucratic incompetence, social repression, exploitation, populist bigotry, state violence, and what it means to do good within a system that rewards bad people. It's a story about an individual, stuck within that system, who must make hard choices as they redefine to themselves and those around them what it means to find justice. It's a story set in a gilded world where there is hope and opportunity to change things, but can only be achieved when fought for by the many.

Rookie Sentinel Receives Commendation

By Albert Porter

In a pomp filled ceremony Wednesday, the Sentinel Corps celebrated a number of their ranks. The most notable of these was an award for Valor given to green Corps member Edwin Leyendecker.

Before bestowing the medal upon Leyendecker's neck, Commissioner Dreyfus regailed the rookie's short but exceptional tenure. From stopping an armed robbery without the use of force, to assisting Carolinian refugees after the terrible complex fire, to the latest event—the one for which he has received his award—where he valiantly responded to an emergency call from one of his fellow Sentinels. While the Sentinel, Oscar Wallace, ultimately perished from his gunshot wounds, Leyendecker's quick response and strategic thinking prevented further death and uncovered key evidence which has allowed the Corps to pursue the main suspect of this heneious act of murder.

Vets Protest for Ninth Straight Day

By Emma Wright

Michael Julius puts his military discipline and organizational skills to work every day. Not, however, for money, but as part of a grassroots volunteer effort to support his fellow comrades whom he fought alongside in the Astorian War. Julius is one of the many veterans now organizing against what they call "an utter disdain" toward those who fought for the Free Cities and have come home to what they see as "innumerable broken promises" from the Mayor and city.

For nine days, now, Astorian vets have gathered in front of City Hall and the Mayor's Residence demnanding adequate compensation for their time served. Many have said that the city has withheld promised bonus pay, job opportunities, housing, and even medical assistance.

"All we receive are excuses and obfuscation," said protest member Hugo Vásquez. "They treat us as disposable," he went on to say. Vásquez served as an Aviaral Munitions Officer aboard the FCS Emancipation during the war, receiving commendations from his superiors for his dedicated service. He decried the lack of accountability, and wants "real action" to make things right.

Moffet Ships "Greatest Innovation of Century" Says Committee

By Edith MacCindy

It's official, during the annual meeting of the International Committee on Science and Progress this year, the Committee's members have declared Charles Moffett's airship design to be the "Greatest Innovation of the 20th Century, and Perhaps One of All Time".

The Committee, which met at the Geatrik Academy of Engineering in Angaft this April, announced the results of its 34th annual meeting last week. The "Century Prize", as it has been called, is a unique feature of this year's lineup. The Committee also deliberated a variety of contenders for its other awards, including for Physics, Chemistry, Medicine, and Engineering.

Sentinels Deployed to Quell Riot

By Emma Wright

As protests boiled into riots on the Old Foundry Deck late Saturday, many were surprised to see the usual patrolman line augmented with the bronze helmets of the Sentinel Corps. Twenty-two Sentinels in total were deployed to assist the Patrol with crowd control and dispersal, a first in the Corps' history. As the dust settles, a fierce debate is coming to the forefront about the role of the Sentinels. Some are asking: Are they saviors of the people, or merely servants to those in power?

Sources say Mayor Clifton had been preparing the unprecedented measure for weeks, as unrest across the city has grown more potent. In coordination with Commissioner Dreyfus and Police Chief Erikson, fifty Sentinels so-far have been trained in riot tactics and mob disarmament, with another hundred slated for the training in the coming months. As part of their deployment, the Sentinels have been issued appropriate riot gear and batons.

GUEST: Columbia Establishes Secret Police as Liberties Curtailed

By Republic's Banner Staff

Quiet air fills the streets of Orange Bay, the first stillness in many weeks. This is not a peaceful calm, however, as Columbians huddle in their homes and businesses waiting in anxious anticipation for what might come next. While the soldiers, for the most part, are no longer in the streets, the Bragg Regime maintains its grip through more sinister means: a web of sleuths, informants, and agents that have been consolidated under the "Bureau of Domestic Security".

The influence of this agency, which amongst the diaspora has been nicknamed "Domesec", can be found far outside of Orange Bay. From Callumet to New Philippi and elsewhere, Domesec seems to have entrenched itself deeply. Reports from those in Columbia mention anonymous "watch leaders" being appointed over neighborhoods and companies. These agents, it seems, are working to snuff out dissent and stifle any remaining freedoms and independence those living under Bragg's rule may have.

Free Cities Congress Debates Economic Recovery for Concorda

By Emma Wright

In its second plenary session of the month, the Free Cities Congress continued the debate over allocation of funds and manpower toward the Concordan Relief and Reconstruction Fund. Debate over the Fund, established two years ago near the end of the war, has been increasingly intense as the legally mandated renewal date approaches. Tensions are inflamed over the Fund's scope and cost, which has left its future in purgatory.

Two major camps have merged in the latest session, neither with a majority. Those who support increasing the Fund's size, lead by Delegate Richard Modesto (IP - Aedem), assert that the responsibilities and financing around Concorda's reconstruction are best directed centrally through Congress. Delegate Wysteria Parks (CTP - Helena) and others in opposition have argued that the scope of the Fund must be limited to reduce waste and ensure costs are kept low. Instead, they say that financing beyond the Fund's current size is best coordinated through individual member cities according to need and means.

Union Leaders Meet to Discuss Coordination, Contingencies

By Emma Wright

Despite ongoing talks with city officials, leaders of Helena's Teamsters, Longshoremen, Industrial Workers, and other unions met on Friday to discuss contingency planning. These contingencies, which could involve coordinated strikes, boycotts, and other measures, are being formulated "just in case" negotiations fall through. While publicly the union leaders are optimistic, some with knowledge on private discussions have said that the overall mood is "frustrated", and that coordinated action is "more likely than not."

One notable exclusion to the meeting was Ermine Hauster, leader of the Civil Servant Unions Association. Hauster, whose election last year continues to be contested by some members, is a long-time ally of Mayor Clifton and was quick to make a deal with the city when negotiations began. Recent sentiment amongst union members, however, indicates rising discontent with Hauster and overall sentiment seems to be that the union is trading its bargaining power for "pocket filling pork" as one anonymous union member stated.
