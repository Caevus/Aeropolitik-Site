Michael Julius puts his military discipline and organizational skills to work every day. Not, however, for money, but as part of a grassroots volunteer effort to support his fellow comrades whom he fought alongside in the Astorian War. Julius is one of the many veterans now organizing against what they call "an utter disdain" toward those who fought for the Free Cities and have come home to what they see as "innumerable broken promises" from the Mayor and city.

For nine days, now, Astorian vets have gathered in front of City Hall and the Mayor's Residence demnanding adequate compensation for their time served. Many have said that the city has withheld promised bonus pay, job opportunities, housing, and even medical assistance.

"All we receive are excuses and obfuscation," said protest member Hugo Vásquez. "They treat us as disposable," he went on to say. Vásquez served as an Aviaral Munitions Officer aboard the FCS Emancipation during the war, receiving commendations from his superiors for his dedicated service. He decried the lack of accountability, and wants "real action" to make things right.
